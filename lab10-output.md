Part E:
Partner: How come your liabilities are increasing even though expected life expectency is increasing?
Clarification on rising liabilities: ELV is a health insurer, not a life insurer. Longer life expectancy does not automatically reduce ELV's liabilities because the model's medical claims payable is the amount owed for healthcare services already incurred but not yet paid. It rises when benefit expense, enrollment, medical-cost inflation, utilization or the timing of claim payments rises. Longer life expectancy can even increase total healthcare spending because members may remain covered for more years and may require more chronic-care services, but that is an operating-cost and pricing question rather than a direct balance-sheet formula. In this model, claims payable increases from $17.08 billion to $19.68 billion because it is held at 11.53% of benefit expense. Total liabilities still decline from $76.97 billion in 2026 to $75.07 billion in 2030 because the $5.00 billion debt reduction more than offsets the increase in claims payable. The model does not use life expectancy as an explicit assumption; if demographic longevity is intended to be modeled, it should affect enrollment, utilization, medical-cost trends and premium pricing rather than be subtracted directly from liabilities.
