## What the business actually sells

ELV is primarily a health insurer and benefits administrator serving Individual, Employer Group, BlueCard, Medicare, Medicaid and Federal Employee Program customers. It sells full-risk insurance, fee-based plan administration, stop-loss insurance, dental, vision and supplemental health benefits. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 3–6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

CarelonRx sells pharmacy-benefit and pharmacy services to ELV-affiliated plans and outside customers. Its services include prescription-claim processing, formulary and pharmacy-network management, rebate administration, home-delivery and specialty pharmacies, infusion services and injectable therapies. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 5 and 9](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Carelon Services sells behavioral-health management, specialty-care utilization management, care navigation, home and post-acute care, virtual care, payment integrity, subrogation, clinical-data exchange, analytics and technology or business-process support. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 5–6 and 9–10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

ELV therefore earns money from three main sources: insurance premiums, administrative fees, and pharmacy or health-service revenue. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).] Approximately 32% of 2025 consolidated revenue came from agencies of the U.S. government, making government policy and reimbursement economically important. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

## What management changed and why

Management raised premiums across Health Benefits lines during 2025 to recognize higher medical-cost trends. Revenue increased, but the benefit-expense ratio still rose from 88.5% to 90.0% and Health Benefits operating gain fell 33.4%, indicating that pricing and revenue growth did not fully absorb the cost pressure. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 52–55](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Management expanded the business beyond traditional insurance through acquisitions. Centers was acquired to grow Health Benefits in Medicaid and the population eligible for both Medicaid and Medicare; CareBridge was acquired to advance Carelon's care-at-home strategy for complex government-program patients; and Paragon added ambulatory infusion and specialty-drug capabilities. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 5 and 9, and Item 7, pp. 48–49](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Management also increased targeted spending on its workforce and technology adoption to improve service, clinical coordination, administrative efficiency and the long-term operating model. Those investments increased expenses and contributed to lower Health Benefits operating gain in 2025 and higher operating expenses in Q2 2026. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 5, and Item 7, pp. 53–55](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 1–3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm).]

Finally, ELV restructured certain subsidiaries, producing a discrete non-operating tax benefit and helping lower the 2025 effective tax rate from 24.5% to 15.6%. The filing does not provide a fuller operating rationale, so this report does not treat that benefit as evidence of improved core performance. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 54, and Note 6, pp. 103–105](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

## What management warns about

The central warning is pricing risk. Commercial and Medicaid prices are generally fixed for twelve months using earlier data, while Medicare bids are submitted about six months before the contract year; costs above ELV's estimates generally cannot be recovered through higher premiums during that contract year. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 24–25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Management warns that utilization, provider and labor costs, new treatments, expensive drugs, demographic changes and fraud can cause actual medical costs to differ from estimates; even small differences can materially change results. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 24–25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Enrollment and product mix can deteriorate because of Medicaid eligibility redeterminations, premium increases, benefit changes, economic conditions, negative publicity or loss of customers. ELV also has meaningful geographic concentration in California, New York, Virginia, Indiana, Ohio, Georgia, Florida and Texas. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 25–26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Government regulation can change ELV's prices, enrollment, products and costs. Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment audits can lead to data corrections, payment adjustments, penalties or restrictions on program participation, while pharmacy-benefit-manager reforms can restrict rebates, spread pricing, formularies and network practices. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 27–33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

CarelonRx delegates important pharmacy services to CVS Caremark under an agreement whose current term extends through December 31, 2027, creating vendor and contract-renewal exposure. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 9](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Expansion into home health, behavioral health and direct clinical care introduces malpractice, professional-liability and additional licensing risk that is different from traditional insurance administration. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 32–33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

Other material warnings include cyberattacks or loss of sensitive data, adverse changes to Blue Cross and Blue Shield license agreements, and restrictions on dividends from regulated subsidiaries that may limit cash available to the parent for debt service, dividends and repurchases. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 26 and 33–38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

## Known evidence after the 10-K

Q2 2026 operating revenue was $49.826 billion, up 0.8% year over year; diluted EPS was $6.71 and adjusted diluted EPS was $7.45. Management raised FY2026 guidance to at least $20.10 of GAAP diluted EPS, at least $27.00 of adjusted diluted EPS and at least $6.0 billion of operating cash flow. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 1–2 and 8](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm).]

The Q2 2026 benefit-expense ratio was 89.7%, 80 basis points worse year over year, and operating margin declined to 3.5% from 4.9%. Management attributed the benefit-ratio increase to elevated medical-cost trends in government businesses, partly offset by improved Individual ACA performance. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, p. 2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm).]

Medical membership was 44.949 million at June 30, 2026, down 672,000 or 1.5% year over year. Medicare Advantage membership was down 15.9%, Medicaid was down 4.3%, and Employer Group risk-based membership was down 5.5%. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, p. 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, p. 40](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm).]

At June 30, 2026, consolidated cash and cash equivalents were $10.232 billion and total long-term debt was $31.044 billion, including the current portion. Only $2.062 billion of cash, cash equivalents and investments was held at the parent and available for general corporate purposes, so consolidated cash should not be treated as entirely distributable. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 1, pp. 3 and 25, and Item 2, p. 49](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm).]

ELV repurchased 4.4 million shares for $1.358 billion during the first half of 2026 at an average price of $310.89, leaving $5.337 billion authorized at June 30. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 1, Note 12, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm).]

CEO Gail Boudreaux purchased 2,725 ELV shares in two open-market transactions on July 17, 2026 for approximately $1.002 million in total, and director Ramiro Peru purchased 1,000 shares for $366,050 on the same date. [Source: [Gail Boudreaux SEC Form 4, Table I, filed July 17, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000128792026000006/xslF345X03/wk-form4_1784310641.xml); [Ramiro Peru SEC Form 4, Table I, filed July 17, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000119204526000004/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1784315092.xml); totals are arithmetic from the reported shares and prices.]

## Why the call remains watch-defer

The positive side is management's minimum $27.00 adjusted-EPS guidance, at least $6.0 billion of operating-cash-flow guidance, continued repurchases, open-market insider purchases and growth in Carelon's integrated service capabilities. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 1–4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 1, Note 12, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm); SEC Forms 4 cited above; [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 5–10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

The negative side is the 89.7% benefit-expense ratio, lower operating margin, falling government and employer risk-based membership, regulated cash at subsidiaries, and uncertainty over whether premium increases will catch medical costs without causing additional enrollment losses. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 2–3 and 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, pp. 40–49](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm); [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp. 24–38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm).]

## Research plan

1. Compare quarterly benefit-expense ratios, medical membership and Health Benefits margins against management's explanations in the 2025 10-K and Q2 2026 Form 10-Q.
2. Test rate adequacy by line of business, especially Medicaid and Medicare, and separate pricing improvement from favorable reserve development.
3. Separate Carelon's external revenue and profit from transactions with ELV's own health plans to determine how much genuine third-party diversification it provides.
4. Reconcile adjusted EPS guidance to GAAP EPS and identify which adjustments are recurring.
5. Compare management's prior two years of guidance and medical-cost commentary with subsequent actual results.
6. Quantify parent-company liquidity, subsidiary dividend restrictions, debt maturities and capital-allocation capacity.
7. Track Medicare Advantage audit exposure, CMS Star Ratings, PBM regulation and the CVS Caremark contract.
8. Obtain the committee's actual required return, holding period and risk constraints before changing the decision.
